## Background

Many residents in the municipality live in poverty and face significant barriers to entering and remaining in the workforce. Some are unemployed, while others struggle to find stable employment or lack the skills needed to perform effectively in a job.

These residents may have limited formal education, insufficient digital literacy, language barriers, or a lack of practical and professional skills required by employers.

Financial constraints often make it difficult to access education, training programs, technology, and other learning resources. Traditional learning opportunities may be too expensive, time-consuming, or poorly adapted to individual circumstances.

Furthermore, many residents struggle to identify which skills they need to develop, where to find suitable learning opportunities, and how to connect their learning efforts to actual employment opportunities.

As a result, individuals may remain excluded from the labor market, increasing their risk of long-term poverty, social exclusion, and financial dependency.

## Core Problem

Low-income residents lack accessible, affordable, relevant, and personalized opportunities to develop the skills required to obtain employment, perform effectively in the workplace, and maintain long-term employment.

## Key Challenges

1. **Accessibility:** Residents may lack the financial resources, technology, or time needed to participate in traditional education and training programs.
2. **Relevance:** Individuals often struggle to identify which skills are required by local employers and which learning opportunities could improve their employment prospects.
3. **Personalization:** Residents have different educational backgrounds, language abilities, learning needs, and levels of digital literacy.
4. **Motivation and Confidence:** Previous educational difficulties, unemployment, and financial hardship may reduce confidence and motivation to learn.
5. **Practical Learning:** Traditional training may not provide sufficient opportunities to develop and practice skills relevant to real workplace situations.
6. **Employment Transition:** There is often a gap between acquiring new skills, securing employment, and successfully adapting to workplace expectations.
7. **Continuous Development:** Even after obtaining employment, individuals may need ongoing support to learn new tasks, adapt to changing requirements, and remain employable.

## Main Problem Statement

**How might we help low-income residents acquire the knowledge, skills, and confidence they need to find employment, successfully perform their work, and maintain long-term employment?**

## Target Group

Residents of the municipality who experience financial hardship and face barriers to employment or workplace participation.

The target group may include unemployed adults, people with limited formal education, individuals with language barriers, people with low digital literacy, and employed individuals who need additional skills or support to succeed in their jobs.

## Desired Outcomes

- More residents gain access to relevant and affordable learning opportunities.
- Individuals can identify which skills they need to improve their employment prospects.
- Learning becomes more personalized, practical, and accessible.
- Residents develop greater confidence and independence in their learning.
- More residents successfully transition into employment.
- Employed residents receive support to perform workplace tasks and continue developing their skills.
- The municipality contributes to reducing long-term unemployment, poverty, and social exclusion.

## Design Challenge

**How might we create an inclusive, accessible, and motivating learning experience that enables low-income residents to develop job-relevant skills, overcome barriers to employment, and succeed in the workplace?**

## Scope for Further Exploration

The challenge should initially be approached as an open-ended problem rather than a predefined technology solution.

Further work should explore residents' actual needs, barriers, motivations, learning environments, and employment opportunities.

Potential solutions may involve digital learning tools, AI-powered learning assistants, personalized learning pathways, practical workplace simulations, mentorship, or combinations of digital and human support.

The priority is to identify and validate the most important user needs before defining a solution or developing an Minimum Viable Product (MVP).
